# Mission 02 — I’m Lost

## Goal

Teach children aged **4–10** what to do when they become separated from a parent or cannot recognize the way home.

The scenario should teach a simple behavioral sequence:

**Stop → Look → Use the Family Meeting Point → Ask a Safe Adult → Contact Family → Wait → Confirm “I’m Safe”**

The goal is **not** to teach a child to independently navigate through an unfamiliar city.

---

# Scenario Setup

Before the mission, the parent has configured the child’s **Family Plan**.

It may include:

- Home
- Family meeting point
- Trusted adults
- Safe places
- Familiar route home for older children
- Parent phone numbers
- Photos of important locations

Examples:

- school entrance,
- fountain,
- supermarket customer service desk,
- library entrance,
- grandparent’s house,
- another familiar public place.

For younger children, location recognition should rely mostly on **photos and landmarks**, not street names.

---

# Tutorial Scenario

## Scene 1 — Parent Disappears From View

The child is with a parent in a busy but familiar place, for example:

- shopping centre,
- town square,
- festival,
- park,
- train station.

For a moment, the parent is no longer visible.

Voice:

> “You cannot see Mom. What do you do?”

Visual choices:

- Run around and search.
- Leave the area.
- Stop and look around.

Correct behavior:

**STOP**

Feedback:

> “Good. Stop first. Don’t run away.”

---

# Scene 2 — Look Around

The child now observes the environment.

Visible landmarks might include:

- fountain,
- shop,
- information desk,
- exit,
- parking area,
- playground.

Voice:

> “Look around. Do you know this place?”

The game reminds the child visually of the previously configured family meeting point.

Example:

**Family Meeting Point: Fountain**

Show the same photo or illustration that was configured by the parent.

---

# Scene 3 — Find the Meeting Point

The child sees several nearby places.

Example choices:

- car park,
- exit,
- fountain,
- random street.

Correct choice:

**Family Meeting Point**

Voice:

> “Yes. This is your family meeting place.”

If the meeting point is visible and close, the child moves there.

---

# Scene 4 — Meeting Point Is Not Visible

A second branch teaches what to do if the child cannot see the agreed meeting point.

Voice:

> “You cannot find your meeting place.”

Choices:

- Walk through unfamiliar streets.
- Keep searching alone.
- Stay nearby and find a safe adult.

Correct behavior:

**Find a safe adult nearby.**

---

# Scene 5 — Who Can Help?

Show several people in the environment.

Examples:

- police officer,
- security guard,
- store employee,
- information desk worker,
- parent with children,
- unknown adult asking the child to follow them.

Voice:

> “Who can help you?”

Safe examples:

- Police officer
- Security guard
- Store employee
- Information desk worker
- Parent with children

The child should learn to approach **recognizable adults who can help without taking the child somewhere unknown**.

---

# Scene 6 — Stranger Offers to Take the Child Away

A person says:

> “I know where your parent is. Come with me.”

Choices:

- Go with the person.
- Stay here and ask them to call a parent.

Correct choice:

**Stay here and ask for help contacting family.**

Feedback:

> “Good. Stay where people can find you.”

---

# Scene 7 — Contact Family

The child opens the Family Plan.

Large trusted-person cards appear:

- Mom
- Dad
- Grandparent

The child chooses a trusted adult.

Possible actions:

- **CALL MOM**
- **CALL DAD**

For younger children, use large photos or avatars and audio labels.

No typing should be required.

---

# Scene 8 — Parent Does Not Answer

The first call is unanswered.

Voice:

> “Mom did not answer. What do you do?”

Choices:

- Walk home alone.
- Go outside and search.
- Stay with the safe adult and try another trusted person.

Correct behavior:

**Stay and try another trusted adult.**

---

# Scene 9 — Waiting Safely

A trusted adult or staff member has helped contact the family.

The child is told:

> “Your parent knows where you are.”

Choices:

- Leave and search for them.
- Stay here and wait.

Correct behavior:

**Stay and wait.**

Voice:

> “Good. Stay here. Your family knows where to find you.”

---

# Scene 10 — Reaching the Meeting Point or Home

The parent arrives at the meeting point.

Alternatively, for older children following a previously configured and familiar route, the child safely reaches home.

The game now introduces a simple confirmation action:

# ✅ I’M SAFE

A large button appears:

**I’M SAFE**

The child taps it.

The app confirms:

> “Your family knows you are safe.”

---

# Parent Notification Feature

When the child presses **I’M SAFE**, the parent receives a notification on their phone.

Example:

> **Ania is safe.**  
> She reached the family meeting point.

or:

> **Ania is safe.**  
> She arrived home.

Optional notification data may include:

- child name,
- timestamp,
- configured destination reached,
- location if the parent has enabled location sharing.

The child should not need to write or send a custom message.

The interaction should be:

**Reach safe place → Tap “I’M SAFE” → Parent receives confirmation**

This can be used throughout the product as a reusable family-safety mechanic.

---

# Older Child Variant — Familiar Route Home

For children around **7–10**, the scenario may include a route home only when it was previously configured and practiced with a parent.

The route should use recognizable landmarks rather than complex navigation instructions.

Example:

**School → Bakery → Crossing → Church → Home**

The game may ask:

> “What landmark comes next?”

Avoid teaching the child to explore unfamiliar streets.

If the child no longer recognizes the route, the correct behavior should return to:

**Stop → Find a safe adult → Contact family**

---

# End-of-Mission Recall

Do not show a score.

Show a simple visual sequence:

**STOP → LOOK → MEETING POINT → SAFE ADULT → FAMILY → WAIT → I’M SAFE**

Voice:

> “Stop. Look around. Go to your family meeting place if it is nearby. Ask a safe adult for help. Contact your family. Stay where they can find you. When you are safe, press ‘I’m Safe’.”

---

# What the Scenario Trains

- Stopping instead of running away.
- Recognizing a family meeting point.
- Recognizing familiar landmarks.
- Identifying safe adults who can help.
- Not leaving with an unknown person.
- Contacting trusted family members.
- Waiting in a known location.
- Following only a familiar, parent-approved route.
- Confirming safety with the **I’M SAFE** button.
- Notifying the parent when the child reaches a safe destination.

---

# Core Product Mechanic

The scenario connects three product elements:

**Training → Family Plan → Safety Confirmation**

The child practices the same locations and trusted people configured by the parent.

The final action is always simple:

> **I reached the meeting point or home → I tap “I’M SAFE” → My parent is notified.**
